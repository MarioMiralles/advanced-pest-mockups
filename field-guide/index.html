Plate I · Termites

Drywood termite

Cryptotermes brevis

Also called the West Indian drywood termite. Family Kalotermitidae.

Drywood termites live entirely inside dry wood: trim, furniture, door frames and roof timbers. They never need contact with soil, so a colony can sit quietly in a single piece of wood for years.

Signs you'll notice

  • Small piles of pellets, called frass, that look like coarse sand or ground pepper below wood, on sills and floors
  • Discarded wings on window sills and near lights after a swarm
  • Tiny round kick-out holes in trim, furniture or door frames
  • Wood that sounds hollow or papery when tapped

When they show up

Swarms are most often seen in spring and early summer, usually in the evening near lights and windows. The colony itself works inside the wood all year.

Plate II · Termites

Subterranean termite

Coptotermes spp.

Includes the Formosan and Asian subterranean termites. Family Rhinotermitidae.

Subterranean termites nest in the soil and travel up into a building through mud tubes. Their colonies are large, and they can do serious damage inside walls before anyone notices.

Signs you'll notice

  • Pencil-wide mud tubes on foundations, slabs, walls and inside cabinets
  • Damaged wood packed with soil, often at door frames and baseboards
  • Bubbling paint or soft spots in drywall
  • Swarms of winged termites near lights at dusk

When they show up

Swarms usually come in spring and early summer, often on warm, humid evenings after rain. In South Florida's climate, colonies stay active all year.

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe confirm what it is and how far it has spread. A thorough termite inspection can take up to a week and more than one visit.
  2. CorrectA customized plan that targets the colony and the conditions that drew it in, like moisture and wood touching soil.
  3. ProtectRe-inspection and follow-up for the next month, with quarterly check-ins available.

Plate III · Roaches

Palmetto bug

Periplaneta americana

The American cockroach. Family Blattidae.

The big one. Palmetto bugs mostly live outdoors in mulch, palms, drains and sewers, and come inside looking for water, food and shelter, especially at night.

Signs you'll notice

  • Large reddish brown roaches at night in garages, bathrooms and kitchens
  • Roaches coming up from floor drains or around pipes
  • Dark droppings and dark egg cases in quiet corners
  • Flying roaches on warm, humid nights

When they show up

All year. They tend to move indoors after heavy rain and during the hottest stretch of summer.

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe find how they are getting in and where they are living, inside and out.
  2. CorrectA plan that closes off the source, not just the roaches you see.
  3. ProtectFollow-up for the next month, and we come back if they do.

Plate IV · Roaches

German cockroach

Blattella germanica

Family Ectobiidae.

Small, fast and strictly indoor. German cockroaches breed quickly in kitchens and bathrooms, and they often arrive in boxes, bags and secondhand appliances.

Signs you'll notice

  • Small light brown roaches in kitchens and bathrooms
  • Seeing them in daytime, which usually means a large population
  • Pepper-like droppings in cabinets, drawers and behind appliances
  • A musty smell in a closed cabinet

When they show up

All year, indoors. Numbers can climb fast because each egg case holds dozens of young.

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe find the harborage spots: hinges, motors, cracks and voids.
  2. CorrectA targeted plan for the kitchen and bath, using odorless products that are safe for people and pets.
  3. ProtectFollow-up for the next month, because the young hatch in waves.

Plate V · Ants

Ghost ant

Tapinoma melanocephalum

Family Formicidae.

So small and pale they seem to vanish. Ghost ants are one of the most common indoor ants in South Florida, and they love sweets and moisture.

Signs you'll notice

  • Thin trails of tiny ants along counters, sinks and baseboards
  • Ants in bathrooms, around faucets and in potted plants
  • Ants that seem to appear from nowhere in a wall outlet or cabinet
  • Trails that come back after you wipe them away

When they show up

All year. Activity often picks up in the rainy season, when they move indoors.

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe follow the trails back to where the colony is nesting.
  2. CorrectA plan aimed at the nest, not just the trail on your counter.
  3. ProtectFollow-up for the next month. If they come back, call and we reassess.

Plate VI · Ants

Carpenter ant

Camponotus floridanus

The Florida carpenter ant, often called a bull ant. Family Formicidae.

Big ants that nest in damp or softened wood, hollow trees and wall voids. They don't eat wood the way termites do, but they hollow it out to nest.

Signs you'll notice

  • Large ants on the move at night
  • Trails along tree limbs, fences and wires that touch the house
  • Small piles of wood shavings near damp wood
  • Winged ants indoors

When they show up

All year, mostly at night. Winged ants often appear in late spring and summer.

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe find the nest and the moisture or wood damage behind it.
  2. CorrectA plan that treats the nest and the paths in, like branches touching the roof.
  3. ProtectFollow-up for the next month, with quarterly check-ins available.

Plate VII · Rodents

Roof rat

Rattus rattus

Also called the black rat or fruit rat. Family Muridae.

Agile climbers that travel along power lines, fences and palm fronds, then move into attics, ceilings and garages. South Florida's fruit trees keep them well fed.

Signs you'll notice

  • Scratching or running sounds in the attic or ceiling at night
  • Droppings about 1/2 inch long with pointed ends
  • Half-eaten citrus, mangoes or avocados
  • Chewed wires, gnaw marks and greasy rub marks along their paths

When they show up

All year. They often show up more when fruit trees ripen and when the weather cools a little in winter.

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe find how they get in: roof lines, vents, gaps and branches.
  2. CorrectA plan around entry points and activity, so the problem doesn't just move.
  3. ProtectRe-inspection and follow-up for the next month. If they come back, we reassess.
